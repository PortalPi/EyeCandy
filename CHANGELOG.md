# Eye Candy changelog

## Unreleased

- **Render scale goes up to 200% again.** Since the 14 November patch the game has held render scale at 100%. Eye Candy now lifts that hold each time you log out, so after the next restart of the game render scale can go up to 200%. While it is held, Eye Candy says so, and says a restart lifts it. Nothing to turn on.
- **The window fades much further.** When the mouse is away it now fades to about a third, so the world shows through. It comes back as soon as the mouse does. `/ec fade` still turns this off.
- **Fixed: a quality level after a Look kept the Look's render scale.** Applying High after Moody kept Moody's render scale, resample filter, sharpness and character detail, so the picture and the FPS were not High's. Now each built-in preset hands back what the last built-in set, and those settings go back to yours from before Eye Candy. A render scale override, resample filter, sharpness or character detail you changed yourself on the Hidden tab stays. Showcase and moving between two Looks work the same way.
- **A warning when the game changes your settings.** When you log out, Eye Candy notes your graphics settings. If the game changed any of them before you log in again (a game patch can), one small window lists them with what they were and what they are now. Put them back sets them again and says if the game kept any. Keep the changes leaves them as the game set them. If nothing changed, nothing shows. It never changes settings in a fight, and it takes no backup.
- **Help me pick.** Not sure which preset to use? Press Help me pick on the Presets tab, by Quality levels. It asks one question: Prettier, Faster, or Let Eye Candy pick. Prettier and Faster suggest one preset, a step up or down from where you are, and show what it changes. Nothing changes until you press Apply, and Back to before undoes it. In a fight, Apply waits until the fight ends. Let Eye Candy pick opens Autotune on the Experimental tab with Best balance picked, ready for you to press Start. It only opens when you press the button.
- **Live FPS on the window.** The bottom bar shows your FPS, updated twice a second, so you can watch it move while you drag a slider. On the shrunk bar it sits at the top left, and steps aside while Compare needs the room. It costs next to nothing.
- **Each preset remembers its FPS.** A few seconds after you apply a preset, once you stand still out of a fight, Eye Candy notes the FPS it gives and where. The preset's tooltip then says "Last time you used it: 63 FPS in Goldshire." Moving, turning or a fight makes it wait until you stand still again. If you change a setting first, or don't stand still for a minute, it isn't noted.
- **Steadier frames in big fights, by itself.** Tick "Switch in dungeons, raids and battlegrounds by itself" under Raid & PvP on the Presets tab, or type `/ec instance`. In a dungeon, raid, battleground or arena, Eye Candy lowers your settings to Raid & PvP's, only where yours are higher. When you leave they go back. Anything you change yourself inside stays as you set it. It never switches in combat, waits while Autotune is testing, and takes no backup. If the game's own Raid and Battleground settings are on, the game sets its own graphics settings in raids and battlegrounds, and Eye Candy lowers only the rest there. Off until you turn it on.
- **The Experimental tab has parts.** A row of buttons at its top picks one: Autotune, or the new eye test. It remembers the one you looked at last.
- **Eye test (experimental).** Like at the optician: which is better, 1 or 2? Each round shows one setting two ways in the same spot: shadows, field of view, glow, contrast, view distance, render scale, sharpening, SSAO, grass, and fog and sun rays. Flip between them as often as you like, then pick one, or say they look the same to you. After the last round your answers are on, and Save as preset keeps them as a preset of your own. A fight, a loading screen, Options or closing the window ends it, puts your settings back, and says which one ended it.
- **More than one step back.** Back used to undo only your last preset, and only until you changed something. Now the Presets tab lists what you did today, newest first: each preset, each restore, and your own changes between them. Each has its own Back, which puts back your settings from just before that step and undoes the steps above it. It asks first and says how many settings change. A restore from the Backups tab can now be undone too. The list keeps your last 10 steps and starts fresh each day. It is never a backup. `/ec back` goes back one step, the newest.
- **Photo mode (experimental).** One button on the Experimental tab hides the UI and takes a photo. You pick what it uses: My settings now (nothing changes) or a screenshot preset. The list of screenshot presets starts with Default, which can't be changed or deleted, and holds up to 10 of your own, which you can rename and delete. Click a name to use it. Change puts one on and says so in big writing: adjust it on the Standard and Hidden tabs, then come back and press Save screenshot preset (for Default it saves a new one with a name you pick; Save as new copies a saved one). Cancel keeps nothing, and either way your own settings come back. A photo waits a few seconds for new settings, then says where it is saved (the Screenshots folder in `World of Warcraft\_forever_`). A fight, a loading screen or Esc stops a photo and puts your settings back. A fight or a loading screen also cancels Change it.
- **Steady FPS (experimental).** Off until you turn it on, on the Experimental tab. Pick an FPS goal, from 30 up to 300. When FPS stays under it, Eye Candy lowers render scale a little. When FPS comes back, it raises it again, up to your own. It waits until FPS has been under 90% of your goal, or well over it, for 5 seconds. Then it changes render scale 5% at a time, at most once every 30 seconds, never below 70%, and never in a fight. While render scale is away from yours, the Presets card and the window's title say so. If lowering doesn't help, it stops and says so. It knows your FPS limits: if your limit is under the goal, it aims for the limit, and when the game is in the background (FPS held at the background limit) it waits and changes nothing. It also waits while the game's own Dynamic Render Scale is on. The page shows how many changes it made since you logged in or reloaded, and the longest pause after one.

## 1.0.1

- **A tidy-up.** Old code from before 1.0.0 is gone. Nothing you use changes.

## 1.0.0

### What's new

- **One window, five tabs.** Presets, Standard, Hidden, Backups and Experimental. Presets opens first.
- **Standard tab.** The game's own Graphics menu, in one place, with the game's own names and help text. Each value shows once, settings you can only change in the game's menu are grey and say so, and a line explains the gold dot.
- **Presets.** Save your own, rename, duplicate and delete them. Four new built-in presets for a job: Raid & PvP, Laptop, Screenshot and Capital city. They sit in their own group, Situations.
- **Steadier frames in cities, by itself.** Tick "Switch in cities by itself" under Capital city on the Presets tab, or type `/ec city`. In a capital city Eye Candy lowers view distance, environment detail, fog and far-away character detail, on top of your own settings and only where yours are higher. When you leave they go back. Anything you change yourself while in the city stays as you set it. It never switches in combat, and it takes no backup. Off until you turn it on.
- **Share a preset.** Share makes a short code. A friend pastes it into Import a preset on the Presets tab and sees what it changes. Import saves it as a new preset, marked New, and changes nothing until they press Apply on it. Only graphics settings from Eye Candy's own list can be set by a code. Anything else is skipped and shown.
- **See what you are on.** The top of the Presets tab and the window's title show the preset you are on, and how many settings you have changed since. They also say when Autotune or the city switch has your settings. Back and Restore say what they did right where you clicked, not in chat.
- **Backups.** A dated list instead of one slot. Original (your settings from before Eye Candy, kept for good) and your own named backups. Eye Candy never adds one by itself, so the list holds only what you chose to keep. On the Backups tab the Original is listed first, each row shows your character's name and how many settings differ from now, and Restore shows how many settings change and asks first.
- **Back to before.** After a preset, a "Back to before <name>" button on the Presets tab undoes it. `/ec back` does the same. It goes back one step, and only until you change something else.
- **Sliders** for every number setting, with a typed box, arrows, a tick at the game's default and a reset arrow. Hold Shift for fine steps. Render scale runs in 1% steps from 33% up to the most the game allows, 200%. Sharpness has an Off position.
- **Autotune (experimental),** on its own Experimental tab. Pick a goal (30, 60, 75, 120, 144, 165, 180 or 240 FPS, or Best balance), then what to tune for: Best look, Raid ready (a lot more spare FPS, so your goal holds in a big fight), Smooth (judged by your slowest frames), Sharpest picture (render scale, textures, anti-aliasing, sharpening and view distance first) or Cool and quiet (FPS capped at your goal, for less heat and noise). Then where to start: the lowest settings, or any preset (a quality level, a Look or your own). From a preset, it keeps what makes that preset itself, like Moody's fog and ambient occlusion, and lowers the rest one step at a time until your goal holds. Press Start. It measures your FPS, tries settings one at a time, and keeps only what holds the goal, the slowest frames included. It notices when Vertical Sync holds FPS at your screen's rate, or your CPU sets the limit, and measures the result again at the end. Stand still for about 3 to 7 minutes (Smooth takes 4 to 10). Moving, turning, dragging or zooming the camera, a loading screen or opening Options stops it and puts your settings back. A fight stops it too, and your settings come back when the fight ends. At the end, Save as preset keeps the result as your own preset, with where it was tested and your field of view and camera distance in its tooltip, or Back to before Autotune undoes it.
- **Search box** for the Standard and Hidden tabs, and a gold dot on any setting you have changed from the game default.
- **Minimap button** (right-click opens Backups) and an Eye Candy entry in the game's Options > AddOns list. `/ec minimap` hides or shows the button.
- **Truer tooltips.** Each tooltip quotes Blizzard's own help text first, then says it in plain words. Where Blizzard has none, it says so. Eight descriptions were wrong or incomplete and are fixed. The big one: Depth Effects is soft particles, not depth of field. Resample filter says when the game ignores it.
- **Peek and Compare.** `/ec peek` hides the game's UI and Eye Candy's windows so you can see the picture, with one small Back button (Esc also brings it back). `/ec compare` flips the world between the settings from before your last preset and now, so you can judge a change in the same spot. `/ec compare stop` ends it on "now". Both have buttons on the window's bottom bar too. While comparing, the button says what a click shows next, and the window's title says what is on screen. Right-click Compare, or close Eye Candy, to end on "now". While peeking, the small bar has its own Show before / Show now button, so you can flip with the UI hidden. Compare needs a preset to compare against, so apply one first. Both end on their own if combat starts, and neither starts in combat. Compare also ends on "now" if you reload.
- **Move and resize every window.** Drag a window to move it. Drag the grip in its bottom-right corner to make it bigger or smaller, from 60% to 130%. Right-click the grip to go back to 100%. Eye Candy remembers where each window is and how big, for each account.
- **Shrink the window.** The minus button next to close rolls the main window up to a small bar with Apply, Compare and Peek. The plus opens it again.
- **The window fades a little** when the mouse is away, so more of the world shows. It comes back as soon as the mouse does. `/ec fade` turns this off.
- **`/ec` opens the window.** If another addon also uses `/ec`, the game may open that one instead. Eye Candy then says so once when you log in, and `/eyecandy` always opens Eye Candy.
- **`/ec report`** opens a box with a bug report. Copy it and paste it into the bug form on GitHub.
